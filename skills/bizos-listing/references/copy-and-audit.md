# Copy and audit rules

Read this file for writing, rewriting, auditing, Search Terms, or creative briefs.

## Message design

Start with buyer intent and a defensible benefit. Put the primary category and one meaningful distinction in the title without turning it into a keyword list. Let each bullet answer a distinct buyer question such as fit, use, construction, care, or included items. Use description and A+ direction to explain scenarios, objections, and proof without repeating the title.

Search Terms should complement visible copy. Use only relevant terms supported by the product and target marketplace. Check current Amazon rules before treating any term or field length as compliant; do not invent policy limits.

## Evidence check

Before using a claim, classify it as:

- **Verified own fact:** backed by the user's product files, verified own Listing, or a supplied test/certification.
- **Market observation:** about a competitor or buyer language; may guide positioning but cannot describe the user's product.
- **Inference:** plausible interpretation that must be labeled as such.
- **Unverified:** needs a source or must be removed from publish-ready copy.

Do not invent materials, dimensions, compatibility, certifications, safety outcomes, medical effects, durability, warranty, rankings, or sales. Avoid copying competitors' phrases and protected marks into the proposed copy. When evidence is thin, produce a useful draft with clearly marked verification points instead of presenting it as final.

## Review order

1. Own-product facts and unsupported claims.
2. Marketplace/category fit and current applicable platform rules.
3. Buyer intent and keyword relevance.
4. Differentiation, proof, and objections.
5. Readability, repetition, and requested field coverage.

For an audit, identify the exact phrase or field, explain the issue, and offer a supported revision. For an image or A+ brief, state which visual should demonstrate which verified fact; do not fabricate photos, tests, or certifications.
